## Purpose

**Why these topics?** A major focus of this reading group is to explore quantum theory and compilers through the lens of the ZX calculus. ZX itself is a _new_ graphical way to represent quantum circuits, with surprisingly powerful implications for compilers, classical simulation, and general quantum computing intuition.

**Why this book?** _Picturing Quantum Software_ (PQS) is the ``graduate'' version of _Picturing Quantum Processes_ (PQP), a slower-paced, ZX-calculus intro book without applications to compilation. Depending on who is interested, we may move to PQP instead of PQS, and change material accordingly. Both are excellent books with lots of examples, exercises, and an excellent "Summary" section at the end of every chapter.

**Who is this for?** This reading group is primarily designed for undergraduates with prior quantum computing background and an interest in more advanced quantum topics and/or quantum simulation and/or quantum compilers. This reading group will focus on the key results ZX calculus delivers for these topics. Because of this, we will focus on the results, skipping their proofs in the book.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of the reading group, we aim to be able to answer the following questions in detail:

1. **ZX-calculus**: What is the ZX calculus? What are the rules of the ZX calculus? Why should we care? How do we write ZX in software?
2. **Clifford circuits**: What is a Clifford circuit? How do we represent them as tableaus in Python? How do we represent them in ZX?
3. **T-gate optimization** What is quantum compilation? Why do we care about the number of T gates? How does ZX-calculus help with this?
4. **Measurement-based quantum computation (MBQC)**: What is MBQC? How do we represent it in ZX? What is a graph state? Why does this matter?

## Projects

The end goal for the reading group is to produce some project using ZX calculus or corresponding software. The ZX calculus theory and software ecosystem is quite young, so there is a lot of room to implement something very simple yet new.

For example, a proof of some quantum algorithm and its compilation using ZX-calculus is more than sufficient as a project. If we get credit, you must submit a project to pass.
