# Welcome

This is the ``digital flyer'' for the SUMS Math Puzzles Reading Group SP26. This is slightly different from other reading groups in two ways:
1. We have no single official text.
2. The puzzles and techniques studied may change dramatically from week to week, and each session will be self-contained.

## Texts
We will refer to a large selection of texts, some general puzzle books and others very specialized. Examples of general texts include:
1. Mathematical Puzzles, by Peter Winkler.
2. The Moscow Puzzles, by Boris A. Kordemsky.
3. My Best Mathematical and Logic Puzzles, by Martin Gardner.
4. Math Puzzles Volume 1,2, and 3, by Presh Talwalkar.

Some more specialized texts include:
1. Games, Puzzles, and Computation, by Robert Hearn and Erik Demaine.
2. Algorithmic Puzzles, by Anany Levitin and Maria Levitin.
3. Taking Sudoku Seriously, by Jason Rosenhouse and Laura Taalman.
4. Polyominoes, by Solomon Golomb.

## Purpose

The #1 goal of this reading group is to have fun! We think math puzzles are very fun, and they're even better when you share them with friends.

## Syllabus

**Meeting structure**: Meetings are very flexible. We will start by solving a series of puzzles in small groups. Then, we will discuss some techniques we discovered while solving, and will review some techniques from the literature. Finally, we will try to solve puzzles together using the techniques discussed.

**Outline**: This reading group's topics are very flexible. Since each session is self-contained, we can change the order and topics as we go. We are very open to suggestions for topics and puzzles. 

That said, session 1 is fixed and will be a "puzzle marathon." These are similar to puzzle contests, where participants are given a large number of different puzzle types, and solving puzzles wins you prizes. You may work in teams (and split prizes) so please bring friends!
